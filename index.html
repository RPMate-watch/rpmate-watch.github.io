02 · About the Project

A speed gauge you wear

RPMate is a smart device designed to measure the rotational speed of an electric motor in RPM (revolutions per minute) and make that reading easy to monitor during operation.

Motors rarely fail without warning. Speed drift, vibration and irregular rotation show up in the RPM before they show up as damage. This project puts that signal on a wrist-worn device so it can be watched while work continues.

Main objectives

  • Measure motor rotational speed in RPM.
  • Present the reading clearly on a wearable device.
  • Support continuous monitoring, not only spot checks.
  • Keep the design practical for industrial environments.

The problem

Checking motor speed usually means handheld instruments, a stopped workflow, or no measurement at all. Small speed problems go unnoticed until they become expensive ones.

The proposed solution

A compact wearable meter that measures RPM and keeps the current reading, the recent trend and the extremes visible at a glance.

03 · How It Works

From rotation to reading

1

Detect motor rotation

The watch senses each rotation of the motor shaft as it happens.

2

Receive sensor signals

Every rotation produces a signal that reaches the device as an input pulse.

3

Process the signals

The controller counts the incoming pulses over a fixed time window.

4

Calculate RPM

The pulse count is converted into revolutions per minute.

5

Display the measured speed

The measured RPM is shown on the device and on the monitoring view.

04 · Hardware & Components

What the device is built from

The component concept comes from the team's early prototype layout and is now being adapted from its original purpose to RPM measurement. Final components, circuit diagrams and code will be provided by the project team.

1 Rotation sensor Planned: IR slot-type photoelectric speed encoder module (LM393) reading an encoder disc on the motor shaft.
2 Microcontroller Planned: Arduino Nano-class board (ATmega328P), from the team's prototype layout. Not final.
3 Display module Planned: 0.96" OLED display, 128×64, I2C (SSD1306). Not final.
4 Clock module Planned: DS3231 real-time clock for time and date alongside RPM.
5 Power supply Planned: 3.7V LiPo battery with TP4056 USB-C charging and protection.
6 Watch body and enclosure Hand-soldered prototype board first; a SolidWorks-designed, 3D-printed enclosure is planned for the final build.
